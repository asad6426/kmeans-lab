01 — The Big Idea

Grouping by nearest mean

Clustering sorts unlabeled data into groups so that points inside a group resemble each other more than they resemble points outside it. K-means does this with one simple, repeated move: measure the distance from every point to a small set of centers, and let the nearest center claim the point.

1

Choose k

Decide how many segments to look for — set by the problem, e.g. k = 2.

2

Seed centroids

Pick starting centers, often just k real data points chosen at random.

3

Assign

For each remaining point, compute distance to every centroid — join the nearest.

4

Update

Recompute the centroid as the mean of its cluster's members.

↻ repeat 3 – 4 for every remaining point, until assignments stop changing
